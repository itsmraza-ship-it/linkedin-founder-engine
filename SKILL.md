# LinkedIn Founder Engine — Skill

## The split — personal profile vs company page

- **Personal profile:** voice is a real person ("I", stories, opinions); content is story, lessons, credibility, behind-the-scenes. It carries the majority of activity — people connect with people.
- **Company page:** voice is the business ("we", clear and professional); content is offers, proof, announcements, services, milestones. A steady, smaller share.
- Rule: the personal profile opens doors; the company page gives people somewhere official to land. Never duplicate the same post on both.

Source files: `profile-checklist.md` (checkbox teardown), `content-rhythm.md` (weekly plan + 4 post formats), `comment-playbook.md` (commenting without templates or spam).

## Profile optimisation — highest trust per hour first

1. **Headline:** Role + who you help + outcome. It follows the founder into every comment and search result. No title-only, no buzzword soup. Repo example: "Founder, Epics Deals | I help non-resident founders form US LLCs and stay IRS-compliant".
2. **Cover:** personal 1584 × 396 px, company page 1128 × 191 px. Billboard, not decoration: who you help + outcome + one proof point or clean brand line; large, high-contrast, mobile-readable; nothing important in the corners / photo-overlap area.
3. **About:** lines 1–2 = who you help and what changes (visible before "see more"); then short story, plain services list in client language, verifiable proof, and a close with how to reach you. First person, warm, no corporate filler.
4. **Featured:** pin the main offer / booking page, best-performing post, a lead magnet or guide, and a proof asset. Rotate when something stronger appears.
5. **Experience & contact:** current role first with positioning (not just duties), media attached, company page linked, contact info and custom URL current. Run the full `profile-checklist.md` teardown, including viewing the profile as a stranger.

## Credential showcasing

Add real, relevant credentials under Licenses & Certifications with issuer and date — steadily, **max 2 per day**. Never bulk-dump certificates in one afternoon. Keep a queue of earned vs added, post selectively about major milestones with professional framing (what was learned, who it helps), and never announce before the credential is actually on the profile.

## Posting rhythm

Quality over volume: 3–5 strong personal posts a week plus 1–2 company page posts is a full rhythm for most founders (see `content-rhythm.md`). Formats in the repo: Myth vs Fact, Deadline Alert, Lesson Learned, Client Story (with permission). Draft off-platform, review with fresh eyes, publish by hand, spaced — never back-to-back bursts.

Any deadline content must use verified figures only — e.g. a late or incomplete Form 5472 penalty starts at $25,000; Form 1065 for multi-member LLCs is due Mon Mar 16, 2026. Do not invent a deadline or penalty for a post.

## Commenting — the playbook

Cite a specific detail from the post; vary openings and lengths; write each comment in the moment; zero or one emoji, occasionally; never open with emoji strings; no pitch, links, or "DM me" in other people's comments (the headline and profile do the selling). Reply to every comment on your own posts first, then 3–5 substantive comments per 15–20 minute window, 1–2 windows daily. Never reuse a comment or near-identical wording.

## Account safety — treat the account as a business asset

One session, kept alive; no auto-connect bots, scrapers, auto-commenters, or acting extensions; human, varied daily volume across posts + comments + connections combined; if a session drops, stop — do not hammer re-logins, recover calmly once through official channels. Sensitive moves (connection campaigns, profile overhauls, page changes) go one at a time, deliberately.

## How to apply it

Start with the checklist teardown, fix headline / cover / About / Featured, queue credentials at max 2/day, then adopt the weekly rhythm for four weeks before judging it.
