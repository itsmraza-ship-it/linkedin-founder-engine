# Comment Playbook

On LinkedIn, comments are distribution. A founder who comments well gets discovered by other people's audiences — and remembered. A founder who comments badly gets muted, ignored, or restricted.

This playbook is how to be the first kind.

## The Core Rule

**Add a specific detail from the post.**

Read the post. Pick one concrete thing in it — a number, a decision, a phrase, an experience — and respond to *that*. A comment that could be pasted under any post is worthless. A comment that could only belong under *this* post starts conversations.

> ❌ "Great post! Thanks for sharing."
> ✅ "The point about filing even at $0 income is the one that trips up almost every non-resident founder I talk to — most only find out when the penalty letter arrives."

## How to Write Comments That Land

### 1. Varied Openings
Rotate how you start. If your last ten comments all begin the same way, you smell like a template.

- React to the detail: "That $25,000 figure…"
- Agree and extend: "Yes — and the part nobody mentions is…"
- Share the mirror experience: "We saw exactly this last quarter when…"
- Ask the real question: "How did you handle the clients who…?"
- Respectfully push back: "I'd add one nuance here…"

### 2. Varied Lengths
Real people write a one-line reaction sometimes and a short paragraph other times. Mix:
- Short (1 line): a sharp agreement or a genuine question
- Medium (2–4 lines): add an example, a number, or a consequence
- Occasional longer: when you genuinely have a story that extends the post

### 3. No Template Smell
- Never reuse the same comment, or near-identical wording, across posts.
- Never comment in bursts — 20 comments in 10 minutes is automation behaviour, whatever tool (or hand) produced it.
- Write each comment in the moment, for that post.

### 4. No Emoji Spam
- Zero or one emoji, occasionally — never a string of them.
- Never open with 🔥👏🙌. Founders are taken seriously in proportion to how seriously they write.

### 5. No Pitching in Other People's Comments
- No links to your offer, no "DM me", no service list under someone else's post.
- Your headline and profile do the selling. The comment does the relationship-building.
- The one exception: the original poster explicitly asks for recommendations or resources — then a helpful, relevant answer (not an ad) is welcome.

## Who to Comment On

Prioritise in this order:

1. **Clients and past clients** — support their wins publicly; it retains and it signals.
2. **Partners** — fintech, banking, service partners and collaborators. Their audiences overlap yours.
3. **Peers** — other founders in adjacent (non-competing) spaces. Peer comments compound into referrals and collaborations.
4. **Prospects' posts** — founders in your target market talking about the problems you solve. Help genuinely; never ambush.
5. **Bigger voices in your niche** — a specific, substantive comment early on a strong post puts you in front of exactly the right audience.

Keep a short, living list of 15–25 accounts across these groups and visit it in your daily comment window — instead of scrolling the feed and hoping.

## Daily Comment Routine

- **One or two windows per day, 15–20 minutes each.** Comments live inside your single LinkedIn session, alongside posting.
- **Reply to every comment on your own posts first.** One reply each, specific and warm. Conversations on your posts outrank comments elsewhere.
- **Then work your list:** 3–5 substantive comments on others' posts per window is plenty.
- **Total daily actions stay human** — posts + comments + connections combined should look like a busy founder, not a growth tool.

## Turning Comments Into Business (Without Being Gross)

1. Comment well, consistently, for weeks.
2. People check your profile — which is why [profile-checklist.md](profile-checklist.md) comes first.
3. Conversations that naturally continue move to connection requests and messages — initiated by interest, not by a comment ambush.
4. Track which relationships came from comments. You'll be surprised how much pipeline starts as a reply.

## Red Lines

- ❌ Copy-pasted or Mad-Libs comments ("Great insights, [Name]!")
- ❌ Commenting on posts you haven't read
- ❌ Disagreeing performatively for attention
- ❌ Mass-connecting after commenting to harvest the audience
- ❌ Any automation tool touching your commenting — ever
