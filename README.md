# LinkedIn Founder Engine

A practical LinkedIn playbook for founders — how to set up a profile that earns trust, split your personal profile from your company page, and post with a rhythm that builds a business instead of burning an account.

LinkedIn rewards credibility and consistency. It punishes automation-looking behaviour. This system is built for the first and allergic to the second.

## The Split: Personal Profile vs. Company Page

Founders get this wrong by posting everything from one place. Use both — for different jobs.

| | Personal Profile | Company Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | A real person: "I", stories, opinions | The business: "we", clear and professional |
| **Content** | Story, lessons learned, credibility, behind-the-scenes, thoughtful takes | Offers, proof, announcements, services, milestones |
| **Why people follow** | They trust *you* | They may buy from *the company* |
| **Posting load** | The majority of your activity | A steady, smaller share |

**Rule of thumb:** people connect with people. Your personal profile opens doors; your company page gives those people somewhere official to land.

## Profile Optimization

### Cover Image
- **Personal profile:** 1584 × 396 px
- **Company page:** 1128 × 191 px
- Use it as a billboard, not decoration: who you help + the outcome + one proof point or a clean brand line. Keep text large, high-contrast, and readable on mobile. Nothing important in the corners (your photo overlaps the left side on personal profiles).

### Headline Formula
Your headline follows you into every comment and search result. Use:

> **Role + who you help + outcome**

Examples:
- "Founder, Epics Deals | I help non-resident founders form US LLCs and stay IRS-compliant"
- "Accountant | Helping e-commerce sellers clean up their books and file on time"

Avoid: job title alone, buzzword soup, or a headline that only names your company.

### About Section Structure
1. **Line 1–2 — Who you help and what changes for them.** (This is what shows before "see more".)
2. **Middle — Your story in short:** how you got here, what you've built, who you've served, credentials that matter.
3. **Services — a plain list** of what people can come to you for, in the words clients actually use.
4. **Proof — numbers, partnerships, certifications** you can back up.
5. **Close — how to reach you** and what to do next.

Write it in first person on a personal profile. Warm, direct, no corporate filler.

### Featured Section
Pin your best assets where visitors can't miss them:
- Your main offer or booking page
- Your best-performing post
- A lead magnet, guide, or free resource
- A media mention, talk, or key announcement

Rotate it when a newer, stronger asset appears. An empty Featured section on a founder profile is a missed billboard.

### Experience & Details
- Current role first, with a description that repeats your positioning (not just duties).
- Add media to experience entries: links, decks, certificates.
- Keep contact info current and public enough to be reached.

## Credential Showcasing

Certifications, licenses, and achievements compound trust — but only if they're real, relevant, and added steadily.

- Add credentials to the **Licenses & Certifications** section with issuer and date.
- **Add steadily — max 2 per day.** Dropping 20 certificates in one afternoon looks staged and reads as a profile stunt.
- Prioritise credentials your buyers recognise: industry certifications, tax/accounting credentials, partner certifications, platform qualifications.
- Post about major milestones selectively — professional framing, what you learned, who it helps you serve better. No hype, no speed-boasting.
- Keep a simple queue list of credentials earned vs. added, so nothing is announced before it's actually on the profile.

## Posting Rhythm

- **Quality over volume.** 3–5 strong personal posts a week beats 14 forgettable ones.
- **Spaced posts.** Never back-to-back bursts. One post, then live your day, then engage. See [content-rhythm.md](content-rhythm.md) for a weekly template.
- **No automation-looking bursts** — identical actions at identical times, mass-connecting, mass-liking, or copy-pasted comments will get accounts restricted.
- **Engagement is part of posting.** Comment thoughtfully on peers, partners, and clients — see [comment-playbook.md](comment-playbook.md).
- **Draft first.** Prepare posts off-platform, review them, then publish by hand. Nothing public without a final human read.

## Safety: Protect the Account

Your LinkedIn account is a business asset. Treat it that way.

- **One session.** Log in once and keep that session alive. Repeated logins from shifting locations/profiles are a top trigger for checkpoints and restrictions.
- **No ban-risky automation.** No auto-connect bots, scrapers, auto-commenters, or browser extensions that act on LinkedIn for you.
- **Human daily volume.** Keep total daily actions (posts + comments + connections) modest and varied — a person, not a script.
- **If a session drops, stop.** Don't hammer re-logins. Recover the account calmly, once, through official channels.
- **Per-item care on sensitive moves.** Connection campaigns, profile overhauls, and company page changes deserve a deliberate, one-at-a-time approach.
